1. The data is not what the archive hands you

The published archive runs from 1968, but the game changed repeatedly: a 6/49-style pool until 2004, 6/34 with a strong number up to 10 from 2005, and the current 6/37 with strong 1–7 only from 2012. Pooling those eras models a machine that never existed.

Worse is how the older pipeline handled it: it filtered rows by ball value, dropping any draw containing a number above 37. That is not a format filter, it is a selection on the outcome — it deletes precisely the draws that contained high numbers and keeps the low ones, manufacturing a downward bias in the surviving frequency table. In the pre-2004 years it discarded 1,843 of 2,019 draws that way. This report filters by date instead, which is unbiased, and uses the 1,663 draws from 2012 onward.

Two columns deserve attention. Log loss is a proper scoring rule — it grades normalized marginal ball probabilities, not just the top six — and a uniform guess scores exactly ln(37) = 3.6109. It is shown only for the 8 strategies that emit genuine per-number inclusion probabilities; the rest produce ranks, gaps or indicator values, and normalising those into a distribution would be an arbitrary transform that says nothing about calibration (for all-negative scores it collapses to the uniform prior and would report exactly the baseline no matter how the numbers were ranked). 0 of 8 probability-emitting strategies have a lower mean marginal log loss than uniform in this window. This descriptive comparison is not a significance test or proof of future predictive skill. Verdict applies Holm–Bonferroni across all 19 strategies, because the highest of 19 noisy numbers is high by construction.

6. Method

  • Era filtering by date, never by outcome, so the frequency table stays unbiased.
  • Chronological ordering. The archive ships newest-first; consuming it as-is trains a model to predict the past from the future.
  • Set-valued targets. Balls are published in ascending order, so per-position accuracy mostly measures the marginals of order statistics. Every metric here is order-invariant.
  • Walk-forward evaluation with periodic refitting; a strategy never sees a draw at or after the one it is predicting.
  • Exact null baselines (hypergeometric for matches, ln 37 for log loss) plus a Monte-Carlo null, and Holm–Bonferroni correction on every family of tests.
  • Scope of inference. Family correction in one report does not cover repeated researcher selection or monitoring across rebuilds. Prospective decisions need a predeclared protocol.
